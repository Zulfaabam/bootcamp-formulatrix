## Understanding The System

- **"Voodoo programming"** (also known as "programming by coincidence" or "shotgun debugging") is when a developer doesn't understand why a bug is happening, so they just start randomly changing code, adding extra `if (null)` checks, or blindly copying and pasting snippets from the internet, hoping the problem magically goes away. It gets its name because it’s like performing a superstitious ritual—you are doing things without any logical understanding of how the system works, just hoping that one of your random tweaks will "cure" the bug. Even if it temporarily makes the error disappear, it usually just masks the symptom while leaving the root cause untouched (which often leads to more bugs later!).

### Get A Fresh View

- **Cognitive tunneling** (sometimes called "tunnel vision" or "inattentional blindness") is a psychological state where you become so intensely focused on one specific thing that you become completely "blind" to everything else around it. In programming, this usually happens when you've been staring at the exact same function or block of code for hours trying to fix a bug. Because you wrote the code and you know what it is supposed to do, your brain starts tricking you. Instead of reading the actual letters and symbols on the screen, your brain auto-fills what you intended to write. For example, you might look directly at a + sign for 20 minutes but your brain keeps interpreting it as a - sign because that's what you meant to type. You become literally unable to see the typo until you take a break and reset your brain!

- **Rubber duck debugging**: This is a trick where you explain your code, line by line, out loud to an inanimate object (like a rubber duck). Often, just the act of speaking the logic out loud makes you realize where the mistake is, because your brain processes spoken words differently than when you just read them silently.

- **Walking away from the problem**: When you stare at the same problem for too long, you can get "tunnel vision" and miss obvious errors. Simply taking a 10-minute break to grab a coffee or go for a walk lets your brain reset. When you come back, the solution is often immediately obvious with a fresh pair of eyes!
